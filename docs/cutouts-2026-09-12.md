# The piece, isolated — 12 September 2026

**Owner:** *"every product listing should only have the piece isolated. i need
every product picture reviewed and i need their backgrounds gone. i need it to
look like the products are floating in the white space of the site. if there is a
model in the picture keep the model in the clothes, just green screen the back
ground."*

**Done: 895 of 897 catalog covers isolated (99.8%).** 347 MB of seller
photographs in, 125 MB of WebP with a real alpha channel out.

## How, and why not the obvious way

The obvious way is a colour key: sample the border, flood-fill inward, call the
rest the piece. **The corpus was measured before anything was built, and it
refused that approach** — of 40 sampled listings only 8 had the seamless studio
background a flood-fill needs. The rest are a garment on a carpet, on a wooden
table, hanging on a door, on a mannequin in somebody's hallway, worn by a person
in a mirror. A colour key would have quietly mangled three quarters of the
catalog, and the mangling would have looked like a rendering bug rather than a
wrong decision.

`VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` — the subject lift macOS ships, the one
Photos uses — handles all of it, on-device, with no model download, no API and no
per-image cost. It also understands **a person wearing the garment**, which is
precisely the owner's "keep the model in the clothes". Cost of the whole catalog:
about two minutes of compute and one afternoon of building.

`scripts/cutouts.mjs`, `scripts/lift-subject.swift`. macOS only, by nature — it
is an operator tool like the ingest, and it says so instead of pretending to be
portable. CI never runs it.

## What it never does

- **It never overwrites `img`.** The seller's photograph stays exactly what they
  published. `cutout_url` is the derived image; the payload sends the cutout as
  `img`, keeps the original as `photograph`, and stamps `isolated` so a surface
  knows which it is holding (schema v52).
- **A failed lift changes nothing.** The row keeps its photograph and the id goes
  in the review pile. There is no state in which a reader gets a hole.

## Floating, in both themes

A cutout carries its own transparent margin, so `object-fit: cover` — which crops
to fill — would cut away the very edges the matte exists to show. One CSS rule
keyed on the payload's `isolated` flag switches those images to `contain`, drops
the frame, and adds a whisper of drop-shadow so the piece reads as sitting ON the
page rather than pasted onto it. Phosphor and ice each get their own shadow
colour. A surface that has not adopted the flag keeps the old behaviour rather
than breaking.

## Every picture reviewed

`npm run cutouts:review` reads back what was actually **stored** and judges the
alpha channel, because a status of "ok" from the lifter means it found a subject
and matted it — not that the result is a piece floating cleanly in white space.

**867 of 895 clean. 28 flagged. 2 never isolated.**

| fault | n | what it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| full-frame | 26 | the subject fills its own bounding box. Looking at all 26: most are **detail crops and flat-lays with no background to remove** — a waistband, a label, a folded pair of jeans. A few are real: a white suit that lost its trousers, two seller collages where the matte correctly kept all four photographs and the result reads as debris in a card |
| fragments | 1 | a seven-panel Ferragamo collage |
| speckle | 1 | matte debris off a patterned background |
| no-subject | 2 | Vision found nothing to lift; these keep the photograph |

**The first version of the review scored every one of the 895 as clean, and it
was wrong.** Its bleed test measured opacity at the frame edge — and the pipeline
pads every cutout with a transparent margin before storing it, so the test could
not fail. A check that cannot fail is not a check. It was replaced with one that
measures the subject against its own bounding box, and that one flagged 28.

## ⚖ The rights question, which is new

The 10 and 12 September rulings covered **reading and displaying** eBay content;
`lib/ingest/ebay.js` notes that Browse image URLs are display-licensed for API
consumers. Fetching one, matting out its background and serving the result from
ASILUM's own storage is a **derived work** and a different act. It is not covered
by those rulings, and the open questions in
`docs/epn-terms-check-2026-08-22.md` bear directly on it.

Recorded, not resolved. The owner's call. Reversing it is one statement —
`UPDATE items SET cutout_url = NULL` — after which every surface returns to the
seller's photograph with no other change.

## What is not done

- **Gallery images.** Only the 897 catalog covers are isolated. The 7,878
  `product_images` rows are untouched: the same run over them would be roughly
  1 GB of storage, which is the whole Supabase tier. `npm run cutouts -- --extras`
  does it when there is somewhere to put it.
- **Re-ingest staleness.** A later ingest updates `img` without touching
  `cutout_url`, so a re-photographed listing would keep an old cutout.
  `cutout_at` against `updated_at` is the check; nothing enforces it yet.
